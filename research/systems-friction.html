When websites assume you already belong

A short note about why public and civic websites leave newcomers stuck ... and why that should count as a design problem, not a personal failure.

Many government and public websites are built for people who already know the local script: which document comes first, which language completes the task, which office to visit before the form will accept you. If you are new to a place ... or new to that system ... the same page can feel like a wall.

This writeup gathers patterns Jade noticed living in Madrid in Spring 2026, and connects them to the same kinds of handoff problems she sees in nonprofit clinic work. It locks a clear claim, expands mechanisms (language as access, unwritten sequencing, blaming errors), discusses long-horizon AI growth without invented forecasts (capability growth is not automatic inclusion; friction compounds when automation lands on a broken access baseline), and does not claim a controlled study or peer-reviewed findings.
