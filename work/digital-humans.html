Digital Humans

Project Lead for Digital Humans work at Luddy LINK: AI-driven virtual personas aimed at business workflows, not avatar demos for their own sake. Core question: when an AI system looks and communicates like a person, what makes people trust it, use it, and keep using it?

01 · Project Overview

Project Overview

Problem statement

Organisations are putting conversational and person-like AI into customer and employee workflows. Looking human is not the same as earning trust. Disclosure, expectations, oversight, and usability decide whether people adopt a Digital Human ... or abandon it after one awkward turn. My lead work asks that question in a business-facing frame: useful interfaces, not spectacle.

Dates
February 2025 to present.
Team / role
Project Lead (also framed as Digital Humans Team Lead / Project Lead in hire copy). Work sits at Luddy LINK. Team size and internal lab detail stay high-level on this public page.
What the work is for
Clarify how AI-driven virtual personas can fit business workflows: service delivery, staff tools, and organisational adoption ... with trust, disclosure, and human oversight kept explicit.
Jade’s part (Project Lead)

02 · Organisation / Context

Organisation / Context

Organisation: Luddy LINK at the Luddy School of Informatics, Computing, and Engineering, Indiana University Bloomington. This is campus research / lead work on Digital Humans (AI-driven virtual personas), not a municipal client build and not Serve IT.

1. What the organisation needs

Clear thinking about person-like AI in business settings: when disclosure helps, when anthropomorphism misleads, and what “useful” means for customers and staff who have to live with the system after the demo.

2. The challenge

Generative AI use is widespread, and at-scale integration remains rare (McKinsey 2025 · MIT 2025 · research stats). Customer-service leaders are racing toward conversational generative AI while public concern stays high (Gartner; Pew Research Center). The challenge is adoption with accountability ... not another interface that only looks friendly.

3. Process now underway

Frame the research questions · map business workflow touchpoints · study trust / disclosure / HCI (human-computer interaction) literature at a working level · keep public claims honest. Detail in Process / Approach.

4. Intended outcome (honest scope)

In progress: a lead-owned case for Digital Humans as business interfaces, with process visible here and artifacts added when they are public-safe. This page does not claim peer-reviewed findings, product launches, or lab metrics.

06 · Final Reflection

Final Reflection

Mid-stream reflection that still reads complete for hiring: what I own as Project Lead, what I refuse to overclaim, and why Digital Humans sit at the centre of my story.

What I know now

Person-like AI raises the bar for honesty. If the interface looks and talks like a person, disclosure and oversight are product requirements, not footnotes. The restaurant-floor standard still applies: the system has to hold under real use.

Challenges

Keeping public copy useful without leaking unpublished work. Separating field knowledge (adoption and concern are both high) from project-specific results that do not exist on this page yet.

What I would improve

Earlier public-safe artifacts (framing memo, workflow map) so recruiters can see process without waiting for a final paper that may never be the right public vehicle.

Ethical line

No clinical or IRB raw framing on this site. No fake metrics. Accountability stays human even when the interface looks human.
