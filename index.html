Digital Humans as business interfaces

I lead Digital Humans work at Luddy LINK: AI-driven virtual personas for real workflows, not avatar demos for their own sake. Core question: when an AI system looks and communicates like a person, what makes people trust it, use it, and keep using it?

Digital Humans case study About

Why this work matters now

Published market figures (not personal metrics) put the question in context. 72% of businesses use generative AI, up from 37% in 2023 (McKinsey, 2025), yet only 5% of enterprises integrate AI at scale (MIT, 2025). That is a 67 percentage-point adoption-to-scale gap on those inputs ... the unglamorous stretch after the demo, where Digital Humans either earn a place in the workflow or stay a pilot slide.

Trust is already under pressure. 51% report negative AI consequences, led by inaccuracy (Master of Code, 2026). Looking human raises the bar for honesty: disclosure, expectation-setting, and easy override. Only 20% of consumers say providers are very clear on data use (Deloitte, 2025) ... an 80 percentage-point clarity deficit if “very clear” is the bar. 91% consent to terms without reading (Deloitte), so privacy UX that assumes careful reading will fail when a person-like interface asks for trust.

Outside the enterprise boardroom the same tension shows up. Half of US adults feel more concerned than excited about AI in daily life (Pew Research Center). 85% of customer service leaders planned conversational generative AI pilots in 2025 (Gartner), while only 13.5% of EU enterprises (10+ employees) used AI in 2024 (Eurostat). Scale and trust are not the same problem. Digital Humans work sits where they meet.
